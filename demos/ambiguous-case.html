The Ambiguous Case: why SSA can give 0, 1, or 2 triangles

Things to try

  1. Start at Two triangles. Slowly drag a down. Watch the two landing spots move toward each other. What happens right when they meet?
  2. Keep dragging a down past that point. What does sin B do? Why can't there be a triangle?
  3. Go back to two triangles and drag a up until it's longer than b. Where does the second landing spot go?
  4. Make angle A obtuse (over 90°). Can you ever get two triangles? Why not?
  5. In the two-triangle case, compare B1 and B2. What do they always add up to?
